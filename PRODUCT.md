# doesbradpittdie.com

register: brand

## Product purpose
A single-question reference: does Brad Pitt's character die in this film or show? Every credited
appearance (83 titles, 1987 to 2026) is catalogued and sealed. The visitor guesses or reveals the
answer, then can go deeper (the moment, the scene) one curtain at a time. It is doesthedogdie.com
for one actor, built as a premium cinema archive rather than a forum.

## Users
Film fans and the people who watch with them. Two kinds: those who cannot enjoy a film until they
know the lead survives, and those who want to test how well they know Pitt's career. Mostly on
phones, in the evening, deciding what to watch. Ambient light is low; the screen is the brightest
thing in the room.

## Brand voice
Deadpan, cinematic, a little conspiratorial. Three physical-object words: a velvet rope, a case
file, a marquee bulb. Copy is short and plain. It never winks with exclamation marks.

## Anti-references
- Editorial-serif-italic "magazine" aesthetics with hairline rules and lowercase tracked labels.
  The owner's verdict on that version: "feels too AI."
- Warm cream backgrounds, terracotta accents, glassmorphism, gradient text.
- Fan-wiki density; lists of trivia.
- Anything that spoils by accident. Answers are never visible without a deliberate action.

## Reference to move toward
The owner's own restyle at brad-pitt-cinema.bruschiontap.chatgpt.site: navy-black ground, a
bold condensed uppercase headline with the last word in warm orange, small square logo mark,
prominent search bar, a spotlight carousel on large publicity stills, a tight five-column poster
grid where every card asks "Does he make it?" in a boxed panel with two buttons, decade and sort
controls, and a closing call to the spoiler ledger.

## Strategic principles
1. The answer is the product. Everything else is staging for the reveal.
2. Guessing is the loop. Every surface offers a guess before a reveal.
3. Posters are the constant, stills are the reward. Imagery never spoils.
4. One accent color, used for decisions: the answer word, the primary action, the current slide.
5. Motion answers the visitor's action (curtain, stamp, iris). Idle motion is one slow drift.
